# Echo prediction from the board layout

Generated by `firmware/tools/echo_predict.py` from `ESP32S3_Audio_Cam_POE.kicad_pcb`.

Speaker taken at (64.4, 91.1) mm on the board, 8 mm in front of it (over its connector J10).

| Mic | Position (mm) | Distance to speaker | Direct-path delay | Level vs nearer mic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U16 (MIC.R) | 64.0, 55.8 | 36.2 mm | 0.105 ms (1.7 samples) | -0.0 dB |
| U17 (MIC.L) | 136.7, 55.6 | 80.9 mm | 0.236 ms (3.8 samples) | -7.0 dB |

Mic spacing: 72.6 mm. A two-mic beam only works cleanly below c / (2 x spacing) = 2361 Hz; above that
it forms false lobes. So for full-band speech the pair is better used as two mics to choose between (or a
low-band beam plus the better mic above 2361 Hz) than as a full-band beamformer. A beam over the whole
8 kHz band needs mics at most 21 mm apart.

What it means:

- The direct path is well under a millisecond, so it doesn't set the echo canceller's delay: the
  I2S and DMA buffering does (measured by auto-tune). The canceller's length is set by the room's reverb.
- U17 (MIC.L) hears the speaker 7.0 dB less than U16 (MIC.R), so it's the better mic to send from while the speaker
  plays, and the beam should null towards the speaker side.
- Mounting the speaker in the case away from U16 (lower centre, facing the room) cuts the
  near mic's echo; this model takes `--speaker X,Y` to compare positions.
- Structure-borne sound through the case and PCB isn't in this model; the case's separate
  speaker and mic chambers (roadmap 1.19) are what keep it down.

## Simulated results (ic-selftest, classroom preset, NLMS 2048 taps)

| Mic | Echo reduction (ERLE) | Echo left |
|---|---|---|
| U16, 36 mm from the speaker | 16.2 dB | -71.2 dBFS |
| U17, 81 mm from the speaker | 11.8 dB | -70.0 dBFS |

In a classroom the room's reverb dominates the echo, so the far mic gains almost nothing: which mic sends, or
where the speaker sits in the case, matters less than the canceller and the room. The direct path is under
0.3 ms either way.

## Echo between rooms (own voice coming back)

A neighbouring room plays your voice, and its speaker leaks back into your mic. In the simulator:

| Coupling between the rooms | Your voice sent back | Delay |
|---|---|---|
| Through a wall (-20 dB) | 19.9 dB below your voice | 55 ms |
| Open door (-10 dB) | 9.5 dB below your voice | 55 ms |

The echo level follows the coupling between the rooms one for one. The delay is the network plus the
listener's jitter buffer (55 ms in the simulator); real boards add the speaker's buffering, so expect about
75-100 ms. Through a wall it is about 20 dB down, mostly masked by room noise; through an open door it is a
noticeable slap-back.

An own-voice canceller (an NLMS canceller whose reference is the unit's own sent audio) was tried and made
it worse (-9.7 dB against -19.9 dB without): the reference is the canceller's own past output, the classic
feedback-cancellation problem. It was removed. What would work: a feedback canceller with decorrelation (as in
PA systems), or simpler, rooms that hear each other (from the coupling map) play a nearby talker a few dB
quieter, which lowers the echo by the same amount.
