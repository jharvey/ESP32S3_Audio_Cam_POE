# Network load simulation: results

Generated by `firmware/sim/build/ic-netsim.exe` (source: [firmware/sim/netsim/netsim.cpp](../firmware/sim/netsim/netsim.cpp)), 2026-11-12.

Every unit runs the real intercom core. The network is modelled packet by packet: every link has a bit rate and a finite buffer, and optional audio priority (QoS) applies at every queue. In an emergency every camera streams MJPEG to the NVR, as dummy frames of the real size and rate split into 1400-byte packets. Four people hold TALK throughout.

## Model

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| Wired units | 35, on 3 floor PoE switches |
| Unit send ceiling | 15 Mbit/s (W5500 over SPI) |
| Unit switch ports | 100 Mbit/s |
| Core switch | floor uplinks, NVR port, office console port |
| Wi-Fi units | 3, on one access point: 20 Mbit/s each, 1 % packet loss |
| Switch buffer | 128 KB per egress port |
| Jitter buffer | 60 ms; audio arriving later than that is a dropout |

## What it means for the design

1. **Audio must go out ahead of video inside each unit. This is the one that matters.**
   - **The cause is the unit itself, not the network.** A talking unit's voice waits behind its own camera frame in its 15 Mbit/s send queue. That costs 18 ms at VGA and 30 ms with heavier VGA frames.
   - **Where it breaks:** with 720p-size frames the delay reaches 61 ms, past the 60 ms jitter buffer, and 6.9 % of audio frames become dropouts.
   - **The fix:** with audio priority, latency stays around 1 ms in every case.
   - **Firmware rule:** never queue more than one video packet ahead of audio, or pace video in small slices. Also mark audio DSCP EF so switches that honour QoS prioritise it too.
2. **The NVR port must be gigabit.**
   - All 35 cameras at VGA 12 fps is about 103 Mbit/s. A 100 Mbit/s NVR port loses about 31 % of video frames.
   - Audio is not affected, because it never crosses that port.
3. **100 Mbit/s floor uplinks are enough** for VGA video plus audio: 37 % peak. Gigabit uplinks leave headroom for 720p, the console and future use.
4. **Wi-Fi units:** about 0.1 % audio loss from the 1 % Wi-Fi packet loss, which the jitter buffer conceals. Video from them is fine at VGA.
5. **Normal days** (no video) use about 2 % of any link.

## Not modelled yet

- **Audio codec:** Opus would cut audio about 10×. Audio is raw PCM here.
- **Console viewing:** the console watching cameras through the NVR.
- **Units sharing a switch:** several units sharing one PoE switch's buffer.
- **Wi-Fi airtime:** contention between Wi-Fi clients, and multicast at basic rate. These are modelled as independent 20 Mbit/s links.
- **Switch differences:** real switch buffer sizes and QoS behaviour vary by model.

## Reproduce

```bat
firmware\sim\build-sim.bat
firmware\sim\build\ic-netsim.exe
```

To run a single scenario:

```bat
ic-netsim --units 35 --uplink 100 --nvr 1000 --video 1 --qos 1 --fps 12 --frame-kb 30
```
